# Modular Spice Drawer Organizer

A flat drawer is a perfectly good floor. It just doesn't know what to do with
forty round jars.

This organizer gives each jar a shallow, curved place to rest. The glass body
and the wider lid both get support, and each jar can be lifted out on its own.
Open the drawer, find the cumin, carry on cooking.

![The finished drawer: forty spice jars, each in its own curved cradle](docs/images/completed-drawer.png)

Made by **JKim Makes** for **Ball 4 oz regular-mouth Quilted Crystal jelly jars
(SKU 1440080400)**. That's five jars across and eight from front to back.

## Features

- Jar-shaped cradles support the tapered glass body and wider lid. Each jar
  lifts out on its own.
- Continuous curved panels fill the space between the jar seats, with small
  seams where the panels meet.
- Panels from 1×1 through 3×3 join side to side and front to back with drop-in
  keys. No glue, screws, or extra hardware.
- Universal panels can grow with the collection; matching plugs close unused
  sockets around the outside.
- Removable panels come apart for cleaning and access to the drawer underneath.
- The layout planner uses your drawer and printer dimensions to generate STLs,
  connector counts, and an assembly map for the same Ball jar profile.
- Print flat at 100% scale, with no supports.

## Big drawer, small printer

The finished tray is larger than a home printer's bed, so it starts life as a
few smaller panels. Together they make a cupped floor that follows the shape
of the jars, with small seams where the panels meet. Lift a panel out and you
can get at the drawer underneath, too.

![One printed panel lifted out to show how the tray comes apart](docs/images/removable-panel.png)

The little bow-tie pieces do the joining. Each rounded end sits in a socket
on a neighbouring panel. Drop a key in from above and the panels stay lined
up; lift it out and they can separate. The top of the key follows the cradle's
contour, filling in its small piece of the floor.

![A bow-tie joining key and the sockets it fits into](docs/images/joining-key.png)

For a growing collection, the universal panels have sockets on every edge.
Add another panel across the drawer or behind the last row. Small plugs fill
the unused sockets around the outside until they're needed.

## Make one for your drawer

The [Printables page](https://www.printables.com/model/1879923-modular-spice-drawer-organizer-ball-4-oz-jars)
has the universal panel STLs, keys, plugs, and a one-jar fit trial.

To copy the tested drawer or plan another arrangement:

1. **Print the drawer in the photos.** Download the
   [tested 5×8 set](https://github.com/jkimmakes/spice-drawer-organizer/releases/download/v0.1.0/spice-drawer-tested-5x8-v0.1.0.zip)
   and follow the print plan inside. It includes the six panels and all
   eighteen joining keys. The [other print kits](https://github.com/jkimmakes/spice-drawer-organizer/releases)
   include universal panels and a single-panel fit trial.
2. **Plan a different arrangement.** Measure the inside of your drawer and
   follow the [layout guide](docs/custom-layouts.md). Give the planner your
   drawer dimensions and printer bed size; it works out how many jars fit,
   where to divide the panels, and how many keys and plugs to print. You get
   the STLs, a shopping list for your printer, and a map of where everything goes.

Start with one panel to check the fit of your jars before filling the print
queue. Print flat at **100% scale**, with **supports off**. The
[printing and assembly guide](docs/assembly.md) has the settings and explains
which way round the pieces go.

## Label the jars

The [label-printing guide](docs/label-printing.md) includes editable Brother
P-touch templates for 35 × 18 mm labels in Baskerville SemiBold, plus compact
batch files and the settings used to print them with less wasted tape.

The [one-piece placement jig](docs/label-jig.md) provides a circular base and
an open guide for putting each label at the same height. Print the
[jig STL](accessories/label-jig/ball_4oz_label_jig.stl), lower a jar into the
base, align its oval panel, and hold it steady while applying the label.

![Circular base and label placement guide](docs/images/label-jig.png)

## A few things to measure

The tray in the photos is **378.7 × 499.7 mm** (14.91 × 19.67 in). With the
jars in place, its modeled height is about **76.2 mm / 3 in**. Check the space
inside your drawer, including the room above the jars when it closes.

The current cradle is shaped for the Ball jars named above. A different jar
shape needs a different cradle profile. The planner can change the size of
the arrangement while keeping those little resting places the same size.

This forty-jar drawer has been printed, assembled, and put to use. The included
geometry checks cover jar fit, lifting room, and connector clearance. The
[design notes](docs/design.md) have the measurements and the engineering
behind the curves.
