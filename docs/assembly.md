# Assembly and cleaning

## Print settings

Start with one panel and a key to check your printer and jars. Print flat at
**100% scale**, with **supports off**. The example uses 0.30 mm layers, a
0.20 mm first layer, two walls, 5% grid infill, four top / three bottom layers
and a 3 mm brim. Slice for your own printer and filament.

Print each panel file as many times as its quantity table says. Connector
plates are an alternative to the individual key/plug quantities: print each
plate once. Large generated sets may have several connector plates. The
single-panel fit trial contains one cradle and four edge plugs.

## Put the panels together

![Continuous panel lifted from the drawer](images/removable-panel.png)

1. Place panels according to the generated assembly map. Row zero is at the
   drawer front; every jar lid faces the rear. Panel size is columns × rows.
2. Line up the female sockets and lower the correct X or Y key in from above.
   Flat bottoms face down. The underside arrows point toward the drawer rear.
   Keys have a shaped upper surface, so their direction matters.
3. For universal modules, fit the four types of perimeter plugs according to
   the part list. A left plug and right plug are different halves of an X key;
   front and back plugs are halves of the updated Y key.
4. Seat the jars gently. Check that the bottom and lid end both have support,
   then check removal of a central jar while its neighbours stay in place.
5. Close the drawer gently for the first height check, then test normal drawer
   movement before filling every jar.

The keys align panels in X and Y and can be lifted out. Support a panel while
moving it; the joints are not a carrying handle for a loaded row. If a key
binds, check its direction, brim and first-layer edges before applying force.

![Joining key and sockets](images/joining-key.png)

## Take it apart for cleaning

For cleaning, remove the jars and lift out the keys to separate the panels.
The continuous curved surface keeps most debris above the tray, while the
seams and sockets remain accessible when disassembled. Use the washing and
temperature limits appropriate to your filament. No dishwasher performance
has been tested.

The contents stay in closed jars; the tray is not intended as a container for
loose food. Jar labels are easiest to read when their smooth panel faces up.
The cradle does not lock the jar's rotation.
